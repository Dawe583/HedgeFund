## The headline

**The family is not weakly wrong — it is uniformly, deeply, structurally
wrong.** Mean per-cell Sharpe −2.00, worst −3.11, best −0.93. 42 of 48
cells below −1.0. Walk-forward 0/5 or 1/5 profitable windows nearly
everywhere. Trade counts of 730–760 per cell — the sample size F1 could
never reach — so the measurement is not thin: this is the most
statistically solid rejection the ledger holds.

## What the shape means

A fee-erosion sanity check rules out the boring explanation: at F5's
geometry (1.5 reward:risk, ~1.25% stops) even a coin-flip winrate clears
fees with room. The strategy does not lose to costs; it loses gross.
Fading 1.5–2.5 ATR overextensions in ranging regimes is reliably wrong at
the 1h horizon in this market — extensions continue. Three hours after
F1's basket showed weekly-scale continuation at p 0.015, F5 shows the
same continuation at the hourly scale, measured from the losing side.

That is the strongest information the search has produced so far, and it
is written here rather than in a victory document because the count does
not care which side the evidence lands on: mean reversion at regime scale
is dead at 1h in crypto majors, per-symbol, at every parameter the budget
allowed. Continuation at 1h is the inversion this measurement points at
— measured immediately as the F7 mirror, declared in the program
amendment before any parameter was swept, because the measurement
precedes the hypothesis it generates.

## Second-order evidence

- The regime filter changed nothing. e2.5-r0.35 cells and e1.5-r0.5 cells
  sit within noise of each other — the hypothesis "the filter is the
  edge" fails with the family. Ranging-vs-trending classification is not
  where the continuation lives; the continuation is unconditional.
- BTC loses harder than ETH everywhere (worst −3.11 BTC vs best −0.93
  ETH at the same shape) — inverse of F1, where ETH trended harder.
  Consistent reading: BTC's 1h extensions continue more violently, ETH's
  mean-revert more often, and neither reverts enough to trade.
- ~740 trades per cell at 1h in 23 months in-sample — frequency was never
  the problem for fast families. The sample-size structural argument of
  the F1 verdict does not generalize beyond slow families.
