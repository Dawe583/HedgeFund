# F7 ledger verdict — the first phase-A validation, and the first phase-B death

Date: 2026-11-26 late evening. Family F7 (1h continuation, the F5 mirror),
both phases complete in one day. This is the program's most instructive
artifact so far: the full protocol ran end-to-end — screening, validation,
confirmation, death — and every step did exactly what it exists to do.

## Phase A — the first validated candidates in the program's history

96 cells evaluated (48 original + 48 re-run after the Monte Carlo ruin
artifact was fixed — the artifact rows stay in the ledger, honestly, as
the measurement's own history). Six cells VALIDATED, all ETHUSDT, all
the same D48-s3.0 shape: 380–400 trades, permutation p 0.005–0.032,
bootstrap CI lower +0.63 to +0.70, walk-forward 4/5. The distribution
matters: 83 of 96 rerun cells positive-Sharpe, but only the ETH cluster
cleared every gate — BTC never did. Momentum continuation at 1h was real,
statistically significant, sample-rich, and exactly what F5's corpse
predicted from the other side.

## Phase B — all six dead in their single shot

Out-of-sample 2025-11-01 → 2026-11-26, rails on, one run each, ledger
rows 264–269: every candidate REJECTED with the identical shape — 10
trades in 9 379 bars (vs ~400 in-sample), permutation p 1.0, CI lower
−2.65, walk-forward 0/5. Per the program: dead permanently, no re-entry,
no tweaks. The count is honest: 102 cells for six graves.

## What the death shape means — regime, not noise

The kill is not statistical thinness. 10 trades in 13 months against 400
in 23 is a ~40× frequency collapse, and frequency collapse means the
*signal itself stopped appearing* — the market stopped producing 2-ATR
1h extensions that continue, or started producing them in forms the
entry filter no longer recognizes. The rails are not the explanation:
with 10 trades total the kill-switch never had the sample to engage.
The edge F7 measured in-sample was **regime-conditional**: present in
the trending 2024–2025 tape, absent in whatever late-2025 became. That
is the single most important sentence in the search so far, because it
applies retrospectively to everything: F1's momentum readings, the
kernel sweep's rejections, even the catalog's original failures. Edges
in this market are regime-conditional; a validation protocol that treats
time as interchangeable measures regimes, not strategies.

What this does NOT mean: that phase A was curve-fit. Six survivors at
p ≤ 0.032 out of 96 cells is not the luck distribution (expected false
passes at p 0.05: ~5 — but those would scatter across families and
symbols, not cluster in one coherent parameter neighborhood with
walk-forward 4/5). The in-sample edge was real. It simply did not
survive the regime change, and the protocol — designed in writing before
any of this ran — correctly refused to confuse the two.

## Forward implications, in writing

1. **Regime-conditioning moves from insight to architecture.** The next
   families must measure edge stability across time explicitly, not just
   presence: walk-forward already does this crudely; a family whose edge
   exists in one regime needs a regime detector validated *as part of the
   candidate*. The F5 regime filter failed because it classified tapes;
   what is needed is classification of *when the edge itself is on* —
   a harder, different, and now clearly necessary object.
2. **F4 and F6 remain cheap and informative** — breakout is continuation's
   cousin and inherits its regime question; the catalog control separates
   family death from tape death, which F7 just proved matters.
3. **The data plane is the quiet win**: 524k candles × 10 symbols × 3y
   at 1h/4h, ingest verified. Whatever the next regime is, the measurement
   surface for it already exists.
