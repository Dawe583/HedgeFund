**Acceptance criterion (restated): 24 hours of paper operation without a
missed tick or a state divergence.** Operationally, measured against the
runner's STATUS evidence lines:

- `missed_bars` stays 0 in both processes for the full window — bars the
  stream never delivered AND REST could not recover (backfilled bars are

- no state divergence: positions in the ledger match the kernel's open

- the feed never goes silently stale — feed_stale flips are evidence of
  real outages, and recovery is measured, not assumed

Two processes, not one multi-spec runner: the runner's CLI is single-spec,
and two processes with separate data dirs (one flock each) and separate
databases (no sqlite write contention between desks) is the smallest honest
topology. Heartbeat rows therefore land in each process's own db — the
engine lamp reads the dev db, not these; the soak's heartbeat evidence is
the `heartbeat_failures` counter in the STATUS lines.

## Operational realities, documented not hidden

1. **`caffeinate -s` prevents system sleep** while it runs, so lid-close
   does not partition the network uptime. The machine staying on is still
   an operational reality — a reboot kills the processes, and the manual
   relaunch above is the recovery. A restart mid-soak is not a failed soak:
   it exercises rearm + connect-window backfill, both instrumented in the
   same counters. What it does require is honest bookkeeping in this log.
2. **Settings env prefix trap, measured**: `trading.config.Settings` has
   `env_prefix="TRADING_"` — bare `DATABASE_URL`/`DATA_DIR` are silently
   ignored, and every smoke earlier in this workstream wrote into the dev
   `data.db` (visible evidence: probe rows in `bot_status`/`trades` there).
   The soak launch uses the correct prefixed variables, verified by the
   lock files appearing in the soak data dirs and nowhere else.
3. STATUS cadence is 60 s in both processes. On the 4h tape most lines are
   unchanged for hours — that is the correct shape of continuity evidence,
   not dead air. `bars_processed` there increments 6×/day; the 1m tape
   carries the cadence proof (~1440 increments/day).
4. The scalp-1m boot landed 24 s before a bar close — the connect window
   either made it (no gap) or the backfill fires on the first wire bar.
   Both outcomes are measured by the same counters; the first STATUS lines

```
STATUS {"uptime_s": 60.0, "bots": {"scalp": {"bars_processed": 0, "frame_rows": 1499, "missed_bars": 0, "backfilled_bars": 0, "feed_stale": true, "fills": 0, "positions": 0, "open_position": false, ...}}, "heartbeat_failures": 0}
```

`feed_stale true` on a healthy feed exposed the launch error: the first
launch used `--stale-after 900` — 15 minutes against a 4 HOUR bar
interval. FeedHealth beats ONLY on bar closes (quotes never beat it —
measured here, since quotes were flowing while stale read true), so the
timeout must exceed the bar interval; 900 s guaranteed permanent stale
even on a perfect feed. A fresh feed also reads stale by definition until
the first close arrives (up to 4h on this tape). The process was SIGTERMed
(clean exit in seconds — the shutdown fix holds), relaunched at 00:37:15
UTC with `--stale-after 21600` (1.5× the interval). The relaunched
process re-seeded and reconnected in ~1.5 s; expect `feed_stale true` in
its STATUS lines until the first 4h close — semantics, not failure.

This defect is itself soak-relevant evidence: on the production 4h roster,
stale-after must be interval-aware (the runner's 1m default 180 s would
be equally wrong at 4h). The runner takes it as a CLI flag; sizing it
from the timeframe is the obvious follow-up fix.
